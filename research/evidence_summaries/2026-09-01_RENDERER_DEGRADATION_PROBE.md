# 2026-09-01 Renderer Degradation Probe

## Research relevance

This phase operationalizes one narrow form of recognizable-character degradation: whether a fixed resolved subject state still exposes a committed secret boundary, relationship trust, nickname, and refusal in user-visible output as the expression substrate becomes smaller.

The deterministic result is asymmetric rather than uniformly positive. The zero-model renderer preserved non-disclosure and refusal `5/5`, but did not expose the nickname or trusted relationship tone in any of five deterministic variants. The scripted local-HF and frontier adapter paths preserved all four criteria `5/5`.

This supports a useful distinction between **identity-critical behavioral constraints** and **identity-signaling expression**. In the current fixture, the former survives the lowest tier while two socially recognizable signals do not. That is evidence about where the present degradation curve breaks, not a mandate to expand the template bank.

## Methodological limitation

The local-HF and frontier conditions use deterministic scripted generation while exercising the real adapter and `expression-brief-v1` serialization paths. They show that the necessary information reaches those renderer interfaces; they are not measurements of an actual local model, frontier model, or human recognizability.

## Mechanism discipline

The frontier work reused the existing `ExternalChatRenderer` callback seam instead of inventing a provider layer. It was only extended enough to satisfy the shared renderer contract with zero-effect private cognition. Its justified capability over the simpler local-HF alternative is remote/frontier execution without local model weights or a `transformers` dependency.

Lineage note: transformer architectures emerged from neural attention/sequence-model research rather than ALICE-style response-database systems (Vaswani et al., 2017, *Attention Is All You Need*, NeurIPS 30).
