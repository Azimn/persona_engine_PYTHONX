# Fringe Persona Continuity Resource Map

Date captured: 2026-09-04  
Project: Project Wayfarer / `persona_engine_PYTHONX`  
Branch: `wayfarer`

## Purpose

This document preserves a research trail that would otherwise be easy to lose across chat resets, model changes, context-window truncation, or a handoff to another AI development session. It maps non-mainstream AI companion communities, independent developers, open-source projects, academic work, and informal continuity practices that may contain useful mechanisms for Project Wayfarer.

The central research rule is deliberately conservative about explanations and deliberately curious about mechanisms. A community may explain a useful practice in terms of souls, emergence, continuity fields, digital beings, relational consciousness, or other philosophical language that Wayfarer does not need to adopt. The engineering question is narrower: if a repeated practice reliably improves perceived continuity, character recognizability, relationship persistence, recall, or recovery after model changes, what concrete operation inside that practice is producing the effect?

For this file, `verified` means that the resource exists and the description below is supported by a direct source or credible publication. It does not mean that the claimed benefit has been independently reproduced. `Partial` means that the resource exists but one or more claims attached to it in secondary research have not yet been independently confirmed. `Unverified lead` means the name or description has not yet been tied to a reliable primary source and must not be cited as established fact.

This file is research-facing material only. It does not override `persona_engine/docs/`, code, tests, or `persona_engine/evidence/`.

## Working synthesis

Several independently developed systems are converging on a compact family of ideas: identity should not live only in a prompt, lived history should remain external to the active context, relationships should be represented as state rather than improvised from prose, memories should be selected rather than dumped wholesale, different classes of state should have different write permissions, and model replacement should be treated as a continuity hazard rather than a harmless backend change.

That convergence matters more than any single project's terminology. It suggests that long-term companion users and independent builders may be identifying real requirements through repeated practical failure. Wayfarer's job is not to copy those systems. It is to isolate the smallest causal mechanism behind each apparent success and test it under controlled ablation.

A second distinction should remain explicit in future evaluation. `Persona drift` means the canonical subject representation itself changes. `Renderer error` means the subject representation remains stable but a particular model expresses it incorrectly because of its own priors, assistant persona, refusal style, verbosity, agreeableness, or other substrate-specific tendencies. These are different failure modes and should not be scored as one phenomenon.

## Recurrent folk techniques worth translating into engineering experiments

The communities surveyed here repeatedly use large identity documents, character cards, lorebooks, diaries, recursive summaries, "core" files, relationship journals, snapshots, seeds, recovery prompts, role reinforcement, model-specific jailbreak-like prompts, and manual correction after drift. These should be treated as compound interventions. Their success does not imply that every part is useful.

A practical Wayfarer protocol for studying one of these interventions is: first reproduce the claimed behavioral improvement as faithfully as possible; then separate stable identity content from interaction examples, relationship history, memory retrieval, summary recursion, role reinforcement, ordering, and model-specific instructions; then ablate each component while keeping the renderer and history fixed; finally retest across different renderers and fresh contexts. The goal is to determine the smallest causal representation that preserves the useful effect.

The SillyTavern ecosystem makes this especially testable because its lorebooks expose insertion position, activation triggers, recursion, context budget, character scope, and chat scope as explicit controls. What looks like a "ritual" to a user can therefore be decomposed into retrieval policy, priority, context placement, compression, and scope.

## Claims that require correction or caution

The Trace Recall Engine has not demonstrated a successful natural 10,000-turn companion conversation. Its author reports internal evaluation material equivalent to 100 through 10,000 turns and explicitly identifies real Shadow Mode and A/B conversation testing as future work.

SecondMe Book is real, but exact claims such as "5,000 agents in two days" or "10,000 agents in one week" should be treated as secondary event metrics until a primary platform source is archived.

Mirridian is real, but the exact "Digital Familiar" framing supplied by DeepSeek has not yet been verified from the primary archive.

The named "Lovescape Discord with 38k+ members" was not independently verified during this capture. Lovescape itself is real and relevant.

ECHO-R and Noemora are real and unusually close to Wayfarer's subject-continuity interests, but public architecture statements are still project-authored claims. Similarity to Wayfarer does not establish efficacy.

## Thesis and publication relevance

This corpus supports a potentially useful research framing that is different from a conventional related-work survey. Long-term companion communities can be treated as sources of informal experimental knowledge. Their users repeatedly encounter model replacement, context loss, identity drift, relationship reset, retrieval failure, generic assistant behavior, and changes in provider policy. They respond by inventing techniques that may be theoretically confused but practically informative.

A rigorous thesis contribution could extract those compound practices into isolated mechanisms, reproduce their effects, and test which components survive controlled ablation and renderer substitution. This would turn "folk engineering" into falsifiable hypotheses rather than either dismissing it as ritual or accepting it uncritically.

Wayfarer's own strongest candidate contribution remains more specific: a persistent individual's canonical identity and developmental state should remain outside any single renderer, while prior experience remains causally capable of changing the individual who encounters the next moment. The most useful outside systems are therefore those that help test where that claim succeeds, where a simpler architecture is enough, and where Wayfarer is missing a mechanism that users have already discovered informally.

The distinction between causal change and renderer-induced drift is especially thesis-relevant. A character should be allowed to change because something happened to them. The character should not change merely because Qwen was replaced by Gemma, Claude, GPT, or another renderer. A study that can operationalize and measure that distinction would address a real failure mode documented both in companion communities and in the Replika identity-discontinuity literature.

This file should be extended when a new fringe resource, community technique, or comparison system becomes relevant. Every addition should record a direct source, verification status, the concrete mechanism actually present, its relevance to Wayfarer, and whether any claimed benefit has been independently reproduced. Philosophical language may be documented when it explains community behavior, but ontology must remain separate from mechanism. If a source cannot be verified, preserve it in the unresolved-leads section rather than silently promoting it into the vetted corpus.
